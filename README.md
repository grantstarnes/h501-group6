# h501-group6

Abstract: The main purpose of the project was to identify if there is a relationship between how fast players beat videogames and the ratings they receive. After creating visuals to analyze the results, there didn't seem to be much of a correltation between time to beat games and their ratings. However, the information on the app is still applicable to videogame consumers and producers. Consumers can find recommended games based on details they input and see the results and other games they might consider playing. Videogame producers can use this site to see how well their games are compared to compeitiors or other games they previously produced using visuals and the trend in the different videogames or generes and their success rates. 

Data Description: The data used was from the IGDB database where they had downloadable CSV files with a plentiful amount of videogame data. I used three CSVs from the website and removed unnecessary columns, cleaned up null data by either removing them completely or finding alternative data to input. 

Algorithm Description: The app created is a tool that allows any user who want to find any infomration regarding video games. Users can type in a desired game and find the ratings for each game, their release date, the game's genre, and other additional details about the selected game. The main goal of our app is based on the input the user provides, the app will recommend them games based on their inputs and share ceratin statistics to justify whether they want to buy the game.

Ethical Conerns: There are a few ethical concerns that appear within my code. One example of evaluation bias was during my data cleaning process, I removed the "age_rating" column, which is the European rating system for videogames. While the argument for removing this column was to keep the ratings US based, since most hypothetical users are from the US, I am completely dismissing another way to evaluate videogames that could potentially strengthen our analysis and app credibility. In the future, I would conduct the same analysis, but create separate visuals/results displaying the US ratings and European ratings separately. In other words, not drop the "age_rating" column. Another example of bias that appears in my data cleaning is the measurement bias that appears when I was cleaning the total_rating data, as pointed out by Woods. I rounded the entire column to 1 decimal point to match standard videogame rating formats. It would be better to expand the number of decimals or not round at all to provide proper rating accuracy.
